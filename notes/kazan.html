Kazan

Where Europe meets Asia on the Volga

A white-stone kremlin with a mosque and a cathedral side by side, and Tatar food you won’t find anywhere else.

Sasha's picks

  1. The Kazan Kremlin
    UNESCO-listed walls with a mosque and an Orthodox cathedral side by side.
    Half a day
  2. Kul Sharif Mosque
    Blue domes and white minarets, rebuilt inside the Kremlin and opened in 2005.
    Two or three hours
  3. The Temple of All Religions
    One artist’s life project: domes and minarets from many faiths on one building.
    Two or three hours
  4. Bauman Street
    The pedestrian street for an evening walk, street music and chak-chak.
    Two or three hours
  5. A Tatar dinner
    Echpochmak pies, kazylyk sausage and chak-chak with tea.
    Two or three hours
  6. Sviyazhsk island
    An island town of monasteries on the Volga, about an hour from the city.
    Half a day

When to go

May to September. Sabantuy, the Tatar summer festival, usually falls in June.

Season Months What it’s like
WinterDec–FebCold and clear, with the Kremlin under snow.
SpringMar–MayThe Volga thaws and the city warms up by May.
SummerJun–AugSabantuy festival and long evenings on the embankment.
AutumnSep–NovMild in September, quiet afterwards.

A 2-day plan

Two days is right for Kazan.

  1. 1
    The Kremlin and dinner The Kazan Kremlin · Kul Sharif Mosque · Bauman Street · A Tatar dinner
  2. 2
    The island and the temple Sviyazhsk island · The Temple of All Religions

Getting there

About an hour and a half by plane from Moscow, or overnight by train.

Questions people ask

What languages are spoken?

Russian and Tatar are both official, and many signs are in both.

How many days?

Two days covers the city and Sviyazhsk.

Is Kazan on the Trans-Siberian?

A popular variant of the route runs through Kazan, and ROTW often starts the rail journey here.
