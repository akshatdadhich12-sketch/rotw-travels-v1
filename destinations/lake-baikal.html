The deepest lake on Earth

Lake Baikal

Clear blue ice in February, Olkhon Island in summer, and three nights on the Trans-Siberian to get there.

Plan a trip to Lake Baikal

Why go

Baikal is my favourite place in Russia. It’s the deepest and oldest lake on Earth.

Winter ice or summer island? It’s two different trips.

Highlights

  • Olkhon Island

    The largest island on the lake, with steppe, cliffs and Shaman Rock.

  • The clear ice

    In February and March the ice is clear enough to see the water beneath your feet.

  • The Circum-Baikal Railway

    A historic line along the shore, through tunnels and over stone bridges.

  • Listvyanka

    A lakeside village an hour from Irkutsk, known for smoked omul fish.

  • Irkutsk

    Wooden houses with carved windows, and the gateway to the lake.

  • The Baikal seals

    The nerpa lives only here. See them in Listvyanka or, with luck, on the rocks.

Doing it

  • Winter ice floating

    A traveller says — Floating in a thermal immersion suit in cold water near Irkutsk.

  • Siberian husky dog sledding

    A traveller says — Sledding across snowy Siberian trails behind an eager team of huskies.

  • Traditional horse troika ride

    A traveller says — Riding a traditional wooden horse sleigh through snow-covered Siberian woods.

  • Dockside ice swim exit

    A traveller says — A smiling traveler climbing the wooden dock ladder after cold-water floating.

  • Skating on mirror ice

    A traveller says — Gliding across transparent mirror ice of Lake Baikal into golden sunset.

  • The Baikal Kiss ritual

    A traveller says — Drinking a welcome cocktail through a straw carved directly into ice.

  • Exploring giant ice grottoes

    A traveller says — Walking beneath colossal curtains of crystal icicles on the frozen shore.

  • Taiga snowmobile safari

    A traveller says — A guided convoy of snowmobiles riding along snow-covered Siberian forest trails.

When to go

February and March for the clear ice. July and August for warm days on Olkhon.

    Sep, Oct, Nov

    Golden larches and calm water in September.
