Plan a trip to Murmansk

Why go

Murmansk is above the Arctic Circle. Most people come for the northern lights.

Shall I tell you the best way to see them?

Highlights

  • The northern lights

    From September to March, on clear dark nights away from the city lights.

  • Teriberka

    A fishing village on the Barents Sea, at the edge of the Arctic Ocean.

  • Huskies and reindeer

    Sled rides and Sami culture outside the city.

  • The icebreaker Lenin

    The world’s first nuclear-powered surface ship, now a museum.

  • The Alyosha monument

    A giant soldier above the bay, with a view over the port.

Doing it

  • Reindeer sleigh ride

    A traveller says — Tourists riding a traditional reindeer sleigh through snowy forest.

  • Filming northern lights

    A traveller says — A traveler recording vibrant green aurora dancing in the Arctic night.

  • Arctic water floating

    A traveller says — Travelers floating in freezing Arctic waters in specialized thermal survival suits.

  • Snow tubing ride

    A traveller says — A smiling traveler sliding down a snowy hillside on a winter tubing sled.

  • Snowmobile across the tundra

    A traveller says — A driver navigating a snowmobile towing travelers across the snowy Teriberka tundra.

  • Meeting Siberian huskies

    A traveller says — A visitor petting a friendly Siberian husky at Lovozero Husky Park.

  • Giant throne of Teriberka

    A traveller says — A traveler seated on the landmark wooden throne on the Teriberka beach.

  • Feeding Arctic reindeer

    A traveller says — Visitors interacting with and feeding friendly reindeer in a snowy forest enclosure.

When to go

September to March for the northern lights. Late May to July for the midnight sun.

  • Spring

    Mar, Apr, May

    Snow and light return; the aurora season ends in March.

  • Summer

    Jun, Jul, Aug

    The midnight sun, until late July.

  • Autumn

    Sep, Oct, Nov

    The aurora season starts and the tundra turns red.
